## Brand Position

Bengula Inc is a decision-intelligence and advisory platform focused on
finance, technology, business growth, trade, and economic systems in East
Africa. It helps businesses, investors, and professionals make better
decisions by combining finance, technology, and strategic analysis into
practical guidance. The brand voice should make complex money, trade, and
growth decisions feel understandable without making them feel simplistic.

The Bengula Research Desk explains the systems, incentives, and risks
behind business, finance, technology, and economic change so readers can
act with greater confidence.

[table widths=42,128]
Element | Guidance
Core promise | Practical clarity for business growth, capital access, and investment decisions.
Tone | Clear, composed, precise, optimistic, and evidence-led. A trusted analyst, not a salesperson.
Avoid | Guaranteed-return language, hype, secrets, sensationalism, excessive luxury cues, jargon without explanation, and overly casual finance claims.
Signature line | Adding meaning to life.
Audience | Kenyan and East African SMEs, founders, professionals, investors, cooperatives, diaspora clients, and decision makers seeking evidence-led guidance.
[/table]

## Editorial North Star

Every piece of content should reduce uncertainty for the reader. Readers
should leave knowing what matters, what risks exist, and what actions are
available. Content that informs without helping the reader decide is not
finished.

> positive: The test of any Bengula asset is simple: did it move the
> reader from information to a clearer decision?

## Voice & Tone Mix

Bengula writing blends five registers in deliberate proportion. The mix
keeps analysis at the centre and promotion at the margin.

[table widths=36,24,110]
Register | Weight | What it sounds like
Analytical | 35% | Explains causes, mechanisms, incentives, and trade-offs with evidence.
Practical | 30% | Converts analysis into steps, checklists, thresholds, and next actions.
Educational | 20% | Defines terms plainly and builds the reader's mental model.
Strategic | 10% | Connects today's facts to long-term positioning and second-order effects.
Promotional | 5% | Points to Bengula services only where they genuinely fit the reader's need.
[/table]

## Editorial Principles

- Clarity over complexity: simplify the language, never the thinking.
- Evidence over opinion: claims carry data, sources, or worked numbers.
- Insight over information: reveal something non-obvious, not a news recap.
- Long-term thinking over short-term reactions.
- Explain systems, not just events: show why things happen, not only that they happened.
- Transparency over false certainty: separate facts, estimates, assumptions, and judgment.
- Reader usefulness over completeness: include what helps a decision, and point to what remains unknown.

## Editorial Standards

Bengula content should meet the standard of professional financial education
and advisory publishing. It should be accurate, fair, current, useful, and
clear about its limits. Writers may have a point of view, but the point of
view must be earned by evidence, domain experience, or a stated analytical
framework.

[table widths=38,132]
Standard | Requirement
Accuracy | Verify names, dates, figures, regulations, rates, formulas, and definitions before publication.
Attribution | Credit original sources for data, quotations, charts, claims, and specialist insights.
Independence | Do not let a commercial interest, client relationship, or desired conclusion distort the analysis.
Fairness | Represent material risks, counterarguments, and alternative interpretations, especially on investment or policy topics.
Freshness | Date-sensitive content must state the effective date or "as at" date of the information.
Usefulness | End with implications, decision criteria, or practical next steps for the target reader.
[/table]

> info: Bengula can be opinionated, but it should never be casual with
> accuracy. A confident voice is strongest when the evidence trail is visible.

## Evidence And Sourcing

Use primary sources wherever possible: regulator notices, official statistics,
audited financial statements, product documents, prospectuses, exchange
filings, government publications, court or policy documents, and direct
interviews. Secondary sources can support context, but they should not carry a
major conclusion unless the primary source is unavailable.

[table widths=38,132]
Evidence type | Editorial treatment
Official data | Treat as preferred evidence, but check date, coverage, methodology, and revision history.
Company or product material | Use for stated terms and positioning; verify performance, risk, and market claims independently where possible.
News reporting | Use for chronology and reported statements; avoid building technical conclusions on a single article.
Interviews and expert input | Identify the expertise, role, and perspective of the source. Record what is fact, interpretation, and recommendation.
Market observations | Label as observation or field intelligence unless supported by a documented dataset.
Estimates and models | Show the assumptions, formula, range, and sensitivity where the result affects a reader decision.
[/table]

- Prefer links or source names that allow a reader or editor to retrace the claim.
- Avoid anonymous sourcing unless the information is important, credible, and cannot reasonably be obtained another way.
- Do not cite a source for a claim it does not actually support.
- When sources conflict, state the conflict and explain which source carries more weight.
- For charts and tables, include the source, period covered, unit of measurement, and calculation method.

## Scarce Data And Expert Judgment

Some East African business, credit, trade, agriculture, private-market, and
SME topics will not have complete public datasets. Bengula should still serve
readers in those areas, provided the article is transparent about the evidence
level and gives domain experts room to add informed interpretation.

[table widths=36,44,90]
Confidence level | Label | How to write it
High | Documented | Multiple reliable sources or one authoritative primary source support the claim.
Medium | Supported estimate | Partial data, comparable benchmarks, or a model support the conclusion; assumptions are stated.
Low | Expert judgment | Public data is limited; the view is based on domain experience, interviews, field observation, or professional reasoning.
Unknown | Open question | The evidence is insufficient; explain what data would change the view.
[/table]

Domain expert editors may add their touch through context, judgment, and
practical interpretation when little data is available. Their additions should
make the piece more useful without pretending that expert judgment is the same
as measured fact.

- Use phrases such as "based on available data", "our working assumption", "field interviews suggest", "a reasonable range is", or "in our view".
- Provide ranges instead of precise numbers when precision would be misleading.
- Name the missing data: sample size, geography, time period, product terms, repayment history, yield history, or comparable transactions.
- Explain why the expert view is reasonable: experience, mechanism, benchmark, incentive structure, or observed market behaviour.
- Preserve dissent where it matters. A strong expert note can say, "A credit analyst may weigh this differently because..."

> positive: Scarce data is not a reason to publish vague content. It is a
> reason to publish careful content: clear assumptions, practical judgment,
> and honest confidence levels.

## Subject Matter Editors

Domain expert editors are responsible for adding nuance that a general writer
may miss: local market practice, product mechanics, execution constraints,
regulatory context, behavioural realities, and the questions a serious reader
would ask before acting.

[cards]
Add Context :: Explain the local or sector-specific detail that changes the reader's interpretation.
Stress Test Claims :: Challenge assumptions, optimistic scenarios, and hidden execution risks.
Improve Practicality :: Replace abstract advice with thresholds, documents to check, questions to ask, or actions to sequence.
Mark Confidence :: Label where the piece is documented, estimated, judgment-led, or unresolved.
Protect The Reader :: Flag suitability, liquidity, leverage, tax, legal, regulatory, and operational risks.
[/cards]

Expert edits should be visible in the substance of the piece, not only in the
byline. A strong expert pass usually improves examples, caveats, decision
criteria, and risk language.

## Fact Checking And Review

Every substantial article, PDF, proposal, or public advisory asset should pass
through a light but explicit review workflow before publication.

[table widths=36,134]
Review step | What to check
Editorial edit | Structure, clarity, tone, headline accuracy, reader usefulness, and unnecessary repetition.
Fact check | Names, numbers, dates, claims, calculations, links, quotes, captions, charts, and source alignment.
Expert review | Technical soundness, practical realism, missing risks, assumptions, and whether the conclusion follows from the evidence.
Compliance review | Investment language, guarantees, regulated advice boundaries, conflicts, disclaimers, and suitability warnings.
Final proof | Grammar, formatting, metadata, image rights, mobile readability, PDF rendering, and broken links.
[/table]

- Keep a short record of material assumptions and source files for research PDFs.
- Recheck time-sensitive facts within 48 hours of publication.
- Update or retire evergreen articles when regulations, rates, product terms, or market conditions materially change.
- Correct errors promptly and, for material changes, note the correction or update date.

## Financial And Compliance Language

Bengula content may educate, analyse, and explain options, but it should avoid
language that sounds like a guaranteed outcome or personalised regulated advice
unless the proper advisory process has occurred.

[table widths=52,118]
Avoid | Use instead
This investment will earn... | The target return depends on...
Risk-free opportunity | Lower-risk instrument, subject to issuer, liquidity, market, and operational risks.
Guaranteed profit | Expected return, projected yield, or target outcome, with assumptions stated.
Everyone should buy... | This may suit readers who meet these conditions...
Secret strategy | A documented approach, framework, or market observation.
[/table]

- Include risk language near the claim it qualifies, not only at the end.
- Do not imply affiliation, endorsement, or regulatory approval unless documented.
- Do not use client examples without permission and careful anonymisation.
- Distinguish education from recommendations, and recommendations from personalised advice.
- For tax, legal, or regulated investment matters, encourage readers to consult qualified professionals where appropriate.

## Writing Mechanics

Use clear modern business English. Follow a practical house style based on
common newsroom and professional-services standards: plain language, consistent
terms, active voice where natural, precise attribution, and reader-first
structure.

[table widths=44,126]
Element | House style
Headlines | Specific, plain, and useful. Avoid clickbait, excessive punctuation, and unsupported superlatives.
Lead paragraph | State the tension, decision, or insight within the first 80 words.
Paragraphs | Keep most paragraphs to 2-4 sentences. Use shorter paragraphs for web reading.
Numbers | Use numerals for figures, percentages, years, rates, currency amounts, and comparisons.
Currency | Use the clearest local format for the audience: KES, USD, EUR, or written shillings where context requires.
Acronyms | Spell out on first use unless the acronym is universally understood by the target audience.
Quotes | Quote only when wording matters. Otherwise paraphrase and attribute.
Images | Use images, charts, and diagrams to clarify the subject, not as generic decoration.
[/table]

- Prefer "because" to vague causal language such as "due to the fact that".
- Prefer concrete verbs: "raises", "reduces", "delays", "prices", "funds", "limits", "transfers".
- Define technical terms once, then use them consistently.
- Cut throat-clearing phrases such as "it is important to note that" unless they add meaning.
- Avoid loaded adjectives unless the evidence supports them.

## Article Structure

Long-form articles and research PDFs follow a six-part arc:

1. Hook: a sharp opening fact, tension, or question that earns attention.
2. Context: what the reader needs to know to follow the analysis.
3. Analysis: the mechanism — causes, incentives, and evidence.
4. Implications: what this means for the reader's money, business, or strategy.
5. Recommendations: concrete, prioritised actions with their trade-offs.
6. Conclusion: tie back to the reader's goals; one memorable takeaway.

## The Bengula Lens

Every article examines its subject through the same analytical lens.
Before publishing, check that the piece has addressed each angle.

[cards]
Incentives :: Who gains, who pays, and what behaviour does the structure reward?
Constraints :: What limits the actors involved — capital, regulation, liquidity, infrastructure, execution capacity?
Risks :: What can go wrong, how likely is it, and who carries the loss when it does?
Opportunities :: Where does the change create openings for readers positioned to act?
Trade-offs :: What does each course of action cost, and what is given up by choosing it?
[/cards]

## Signature Sections

Recurring, named sections make Bengula content recognisable and easy to
scan. Use them consistently across articles, PDFs, and decks.

[cards]
Key Insight :: The single non-obvious conclusion of the piece, stated early and plainly.
Risk Factors :: An honest list of what can go wrong, with severity and likelihood where possible.
Decision Framework :: A short checklist or table the reader can apply to their own situation.
Confidence Note :: A short statement on whether the evidence is documented, estimated, judgment-led, or unresolved.
Bengula View :: The desk's position — what we would weigh most heavily, and why.
[/cards]

## The Publication Test

Before any asset ships, it must pass seven questions. If the answer to any
is no, the piece goes back for another pass.

1. Does it reveal something non-obvious?
2. Does it explain causes rather than symptoms?
3. Does it help readers make better decisions?
4. Does it connect information to action?
5. Are facts, figures, dates, and claims sourced or clearly labelled as assumptions?
6. Are risks, limitations, and conflicts fairly represented?
7. Would a domain expert be comfortable standing behind the practical guidance?

> risk: Content that fails the test but ships anyway erodes the advisory
> positioning the brand depends on. When in doubt, cut the piece, not the
> standard.

## Signature Effects

The website pairs the palette with a liquid-glass aesthetic: frosted white
cards over a softly tinted canvas, an aurora backdrop of blurred violet,
orchid, and sky gradients, and a panning violet-to-indigo gradient on
headline text. In static formats, echo this with light backgrounds, soft
violet shadows, rounded corners, and a single dark-violet cover moment.

- Glass cards: white at roughly 62 percent opacity with a hairline border and a soft violet shadow.
- Gradient text: Electric Violet to Orchid to Indigo Pulse, reserved for one headline per asset.
- Dark panels: Deep Violet with light violet text, used sparingly for contrast moments.

## Typography

[table widths=36,50,84]
Role | Recommended font | Usage
Headlines | Georgia or Charter | Editorial, credible, advisory. Use for PDF covers, article titles, and major section headings.
Body/UI | Helvetica Neue, Inter, or Segoe UI | Clean, highly readable, modern. Use for web, decks, tables, captions, and body copy.
Numbers/data | JetBrains Mono or Aptos Mono | Use sparingly for rates, tickers, tags, IDs, and compact dashboards.
Fallback | Helvetica | Use where platform compatibility matters more than character.
[/table]

- Do not use more than two font families in one asset unless data tables need a mono style.
- Headlines should be confident and plain, not slogan-heavy.
- Tables should use smaller type only when spacing remains comfortable.

## Layout Rules

- Use generous white space and a strong typographic hierarchy before adding decoration.
- Use tables only for true comparisons, checklists, or data. Use prose for explanation.
- Advisory-grade presentation: every marketing PDF includes a cover, executive summary, practical framework, source list, and compliance note.
